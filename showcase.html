Flying Salem

FIND YOUR OWN ROOF, FROM THE AIR · START HERE

The ground underneath you is not a drawing. It is the Commonwealth’s public survey of Salem — real elevations, real shoreline, real streets — with 1,706 historic sites sitting exactly where they sit in life. You can spot your own house. That is the entire pitch and it turns out to be enough.

This one is free, and it is free on purpose. It is a small, silly thing we put together at no cost, and it is the quickest way to see what we mean by turning a pile of survey numbers into somewhere you can go. Take the free one first.

DRAWN, NOT CAPTURED — HOW A HEIGHTFIELD BECOMES GROUND
WHAT IT ACTUALLY ISTelemetry, rendered as somewhere you can go — and it cost us nothing.

The renderer’s whole world is one grid of values, one classification layer, and a list of things that stand on it. In this build the grid holds elevation, spanning nine kilometres, ranging −3.2 to 67.9.

It has no idea those numbers were metres. Load population per cell instead and you are flying over demographics. Load signal strength and you are flying over your coverage gaps. Same program — we changed a file.

★ This is the one we give away, and that is the argument. It is a simple, faintly ridiculous little application that cost nothing to produce. The toys are the proof.

None of these is a serious product and none of them pretends to be. They are small, they are cheap, they are for a town of forty thousand people, and one of them is a cat.

That is exactly why they are worth showing you. Every one is a bit of fun sitting on infrastructure built for work that is not fun at all — offline data layers, local models, survey pipelines, structured retrieval, a renderer that turns numbers into landscape. The novelty is the wrapper. The machinery is the same machinery.

So the honest question is not whether we can build your thing. It is which of these engines your data already fits — and that is usually a short conversation, because after a while every dataset starts to look like one of four shapes.
