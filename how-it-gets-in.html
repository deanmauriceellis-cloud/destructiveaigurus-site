HOW IT GETS IN

You never agreed to it. You just never un-agreed.

Almost everything collecting you right now was authorised once, briefly, while you were busy doing something else — and then it simply never stopped. That isn't a scandal. It's a design, and it's the same six moves every time.

01

Consent is taken while you're mid-task

Nobody is asked may we upload your address book. They're asked to “find people you know” — during setup, when the goal is to get past setup. The decision arrives disguised as a step.

02

New features inherit old permission

You agreed to something years ago. What it does today didn't exist then. It shipped switched on, and your ancient yes quietly covered it. You never agreed to that. You just never un-agreed.

03

The grant outlives the reason

You connected an account once, for one purpose, and that purpose ended years ago. The authorisation didn't end with it. Permissions rarely expire; intentions always do.

04

You're asked until you slip

On install. After the update. After the next login. Mid-way through something unrelated. It has to succeed once. You have to decline forever. That asymmetry isn't a flaw in the design — it is the design.

05

It never looks like collection

It looks like a convenience. Find your colleagues. More relevant results. A better experience. The most complete one we ever found looked like a typeface.

06

Nothing tells you it's happening

No light, no log, no receipt, no notice. The only way to find out is to go and look — which almost nobody does, because there's nothing to prompt them to.

Six disguises, one mechanism.

The moment you consent is pulled apart from the moment you're collected. Agree while distracted; be gathered later, indefinitely, invisibly. Every item above is that same separation wearing a different coat — which is why reading the settings page more carefully was never going to save anyone.

A setting is a request. A boundary is enforcement. Asking a system not to do something depends on it continuing to agree. Making it structurally unable to does not. And past the boundary there is only one thing left worth having: measurement — because everything short of it is a vendor describing itself.

WE SHIPPED ONE TOO

Every page of this site used to load its fonts from someone else's servers. To draw three headlines, your browser was made to contact a third party and hand over your address, your device, and the page you were reading — on every visit, before you could consent to anything, with nothing on screen to indicate it.

It had been there for months. We wrote the copy about gathering nothing, and it sat in our own front door the whole time, because it didn't look like collection. It looked like a font. The fonts are ours now and no one else is contacted to render this page.

We're telling you because it's the point: this is not something only careless people ship. It's something you find by going and looking — and then you fix it in an afternoon.
